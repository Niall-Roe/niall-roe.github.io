#### fifth pass — the smoothing rule, found and corrected
You asked me to consider pages 3–6 of the First Attempt carefully, and they settle it. The
correction first: my fourth-pass claim that he drew the class curves freehand and read the
Smoothed values off them was wrong. The earlier draft's ruled table (PDF p. 6) heads its third
column, in his own hand, "The same smoothed by 0.7 each + 0.3 previous" — and the rule
smoothed(i) = 0.7·count(i) + 0.3·count(i−1) reproduces **all thirty smoothed values exactly, in
both drafts** (verified computationally; the example now has an α slider that clicks onto every
red dot at 0.70 and misses elsewhere). So the smoothing is a formula — a one-sided weighted
average leaning each bin 30% toward its lighter neighbour.
The separation is then worked, not drawn: the rough page (PDF p. 4) is covered with mixing-share
algebra — x·5.6 + (1−x)·8.5 chains, 11x + 6(1−x) = 6x + 7(1−x) solved to x = ⅙, small
simultaneous systems — together with two systems of concentric circles drawn through the columns
of smoothed values, which appear to be symmetry tests about candidate standards. The same pages
hold the probable-error bisections (2.05, 1.05, 0.9 → "1.1 ~ 0.9" — the working behind your
chapter's quote and the published ⅝), and a slip tabulating e^x next to sketches of the cut-off
curves that ¶210 of the published text describes — the manufacturing-process discussion already
rehearsed in the first attempt.

#### fourth pass — your text, the finding, and the walk
The intro is your dictated text, filled: the pages are those Peirce numbered 15 onward of MS 427,
published in CP (1931) and EP2; the first attempt concluded three standards, "and it is not
unlikely there is another." And your two questions have answers from the numbers themselves: the
Smoothed column is not a running average — in every one of the twenty rows it equals the sum of
the Separated shares beside it. He drew the class curves by hand over his plotted counts, read
each curve's height per bin (Separated), and summed them (Smoothed): the blue curves and brown
curve of the restored paragraph, worked as arithmetic. The separation is therefore the hand-drawn
curves — a judgement of shape, kept sum-preserving. The chart view now draws his Separated shares
as stacked segments with each red dot landing exactly on its stack, and a moving average is
offered only for contrast.
New default view: "work through it" — the bins fill one at a time as he tallied them, his asides
arriving on cue at the rows where he broke off, and a "flag a standard here" button lets the
reader commit before Peirce's four appear in gold at the end for comparison.
