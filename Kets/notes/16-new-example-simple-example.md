### Text

"Namely, this will happen when the form about which the individuals of one class cluster is not so unlike the form about which individuals of another class cluster but that variations from each middling form may precisely agree. In such a case, we may know in regard to any intermediate form what proportion of the objects of that form had one purpose and what proportion the other; but unless we have some supplementary information we cannot tell which ones had one purpose and which the other."

### Completed

#### fourth pass — the I earns its serifs
The middle pair is now I and 1 drawn properly: at the left the capital I carries top and bottom
bars that lengthen toward almost-square; past the middle the bars fade and the top-left flag takes
over, angling down into the digit. The peak of the letter curve no longer looks like a 1.

##### the round-4 notes this answers

#### second pass — pairs, the split marker, and the drawn mark
Built to your notes: buttons for three pairs (O and 0, l and 1, u and v), each with its own axis of
form and its own way of drawing a mark; the "this form" marker is now thick and coloured in the two
classes' shares; and the interrogated mark itself is drawn — both under the axis (a row of eleven
forms) and beside the readout, so you see the very shape whose parentage is undecidable.

#### the letter O and the digit 0
Built: in place of parasols/umbrellas, two classes every reader already owns — marks of the letter O (made for spelling) and marks of the digit 0 (made for counting). Typefaces draw the digit narrower, so the two classes cluster about different middling forms whose variations overlap; a row of actual glyphs runs under the two curves, and clicking any form on the axis reports what proportion of marks of that shape are letters — never which one this is. Membership is fixed by the mark's purpose, which makes both classes natural in exactly Peirce's sense.
