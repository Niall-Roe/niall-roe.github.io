The method of just noticeable differences

The weight moved in small steps, up or down, until his answer changes.

Here the experimenter moves the weight himself, in a fixed order. A series starts well away from the threshold and steps toward it, one small step at a time, with the subject answering after each. The series ends the moment his answer changes. Later writers call this the method of limits.

Step 2 · the record

The protocol sheet

Written out, a series is a column of pluses and minuses. The crossing is taken as the midpoint of the last two steps, since the change happened somewhere between them.

Step 3 · many series

Both directions, alternating

One series is one number. Run more, alternating direction: ascending series start below anything he can feel and step up.

Every crossing so far, sorted by the direction of its series.

Why both directions Descending series cross low and ascending series cross high, because he carries his previous answer forward a step or two before switching. The two errors run opposite ways, so the pair are run in equal numbers and averaged together.

Part two · the difference threshold

Step 1 · a third answer

Less, equal, or greater

Against the 100 g standard the subject is allowed to say the comparison is lighter, heavier, or the same. A sweep now runs the whole way past the standard, so his answer changes twice: once out of “lighter”, once into “heavier”.

Step 4 · four quantities

What the two crossings give

What this threshold depends on The width of the band is not fixed by his senses alone. It also depends on how sure he insists on being before he will commit to “heavier” rather than “the same”. Tell him to say “the same” only when he really cannot tell and the band narrows; let him use it freely and it widens. The slider below changes nothing about his senses.
