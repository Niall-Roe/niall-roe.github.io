Severity

“One does not have evidence for a claim if nothing has been done to rule out ways the claim may be false.”

Deborah G. Mayo, Statistical Inference as Severe Testing: How to Get Beyond the Statistics Wars (Cambridge University Press, 2018), the weak severity principle.

The severity of a passing result is the probability attaching to the second of Mayo’s two conditions: that the data agree with the claim, and that the test would very probably have produced data agreeing less well with the claim, were the claim false. Everything computed on this page is that probability.

Severity is Deborah Mayo’s criterion, developed with Aris Spanos. The account followed here is hers; any errors in the implementation are not.
